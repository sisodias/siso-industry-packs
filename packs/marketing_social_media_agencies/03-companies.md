# Stage 3 — Companies, capability ceiling and stack tax

Research date: **2026-09-13**. This commercial research was completed and committed before the first Stage 4 discovery search. Supplied SISO inventories were read beforehand, as requested.

## Basis and counting

[analysis] Reference operator: a US, five-person organic-social retainer agency, ten client brands, two Facebook/Instagram profiles per brand, 120 original content packages per month adapted into 240 channel publications. One person hosts sales meetings; all five need internal work management; ten clients need approval access. This is a **constructed procurement case, not an interviewed agency or measured bill**. Taxes, ad spend, payment volume, SMS volume and optional AI consumption are excluded unless stated. Prices below are USD observed on the research date; historical complaints are not current product benchmarks.

### The gap between the tiers

[analysis] Big firms join account strategy, production, identity/data, activation, measurement and expert service. Small agencies can already buy inexpensive scheduling, approval and reporting. They are not priced out of a calendar; they are priced out of **integrated data rights, broad listening coverage, specialist staffing and a maintained end-to-end operating model**. An OSS assembly can attack duplicated operational records and review handoffs. It cannot honestly promise the same proprietary audience graph, media buying relationships, creative talent or causal measurement.

[practitioner] The complaint evidence is more specific than “software is expensive”: a designer copies approved captions through Sheets → Figma → PDF → Markup; another operator must retain both Drive and Airtable; an owner receives scope changes routed directly to staff. These are specifications for versioned work and approval objects, not evidence that every incumbent lacks approvals. Exact quotes and source links are in [Stage 1](01-person.md).

[analysis] **Maximum hypothesis for replaceable subscriptions = $5,460/year**, not $9,419.76: `720 + 1,200 + 1,020 + 2,400 + 120`. Retained base = **$3,959.76/year**. A wedge-only approval-ledger pilot cannot claim the full $5,460; even Airtable cancellation depends on other uses and contract renewal. Actual verified cancelled spend is **null/unmeasured**.

[analysis] Lean counterfactual: remove Airtable from the reference basket and use the existing task board/Sheets well; stack becomes **$8,219.76/year**, and maximum potential displacement falls to **$4,260**. A buyer already satisfied with Metricool or SocialPilot may have little software cash to recover. The migration thesis must then stand on measured review effort, control and data ownership.

[analysis] **Literal “buy every tool mentioned” total is not computable**: ten agency service quotes, multiple enterprise tariffs and dynamic calculators are unknown, and alternatives overlap. It is not represented as a false exact number. The $9,419.76 figure is the complete selected procurement basket above; all quoted fees, API/usage charges, ad spend, stock media, SMS and processing remain excluded or variable. Stage 6 uses only that basket and separately prices the replacement's operating/change costs.

## Commercial research gaps carried forward

[analysis] No customer invoice, account configuration, staffing cost, interview or production outcome was accessed. Buffer's 20-channel volume price; Cloud Campaign's exact included workspace count; Sendible/Publer/Zoho applicable tariffs; and generic alternative seat tariffs remain quote/retrieval gaps. Vendor feature pages establish advertised capability, not adoption, quality or savings. Complaint prevalence, vendor uptime and net human effort remain unmeasured. The OSS sweep must solve objects and handoffs first, not manufacture a savings claim from these gaps.
