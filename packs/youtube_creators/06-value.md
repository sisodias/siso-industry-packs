## What is actually being displaced?

The selected Stage 3 paid comparison basket is **$2,794/year**: Airtable $720 + standalone Frame.io $360 + Buffer $720 + OpusClip $174 + Canva $144 + ElevenLabs $220 + QuickBooks $456. It excludes unquoted music/research/business-email/comms, transaction fees, overages, taxes and existing SUMERA cost. It is a priced subtotal, **not the whole real stack tax**, and buying every competing editor/scheduler in the commercial catalogue would be an invalid baseline.

The potentially replaceable **coordination** subtotal is only **$1,800/year**: Airtable + standalone Frame.io + Buffer. Each term is conditional on actual subscription ownership, sufficient feature parity, allowed account/platform integration and successful cancellation. If the client already receives Frame.io with Premiere, uses a free board/native scheduling, or cannot cancel a commitment, the corresponding avoided-spend term is lower or zero. Actual cancellable spend is currently **unknown**, not $1,800 measured.

Do not automatically remove paid narration/design, catalogue music rights, the accountant's ledger or payment-processing fees. OpusClip adds at most a further $174 in this scenario only after a pilot establishes useful accepted-clip quality and net review-time reduction. Library availability does not establish service equivalence.

Domain, tax, independent encrypted backup retention, restore drills, media-capacity growth, excess transfer, upgrades and migration remain additional costs. Provider disk snapshots do not by themselves establish a tested application-consistent database/media restore. An 8-GiB or lower-cost machine may improve the arithmetic, but claiming that it safely runs this full workload before benchmarking would manufacture an economics input.

**[analysis] Consequence:** this is not yet a defensible pure subscription-arbitrage offer. It becomes interesting if the owner values reliable production handoff, avoids repeated context recovery, can use a smaller validated deployment, already owns adequate VPS capacity, or shares integration engineering across enough paying deployments. Those conditions require evidence; they are not booked cash savings.

## Hours returned: an explicitly unmeasured hypothesis

The following preregistered toy scenario uses **distinct work events**, not three names for the same saved minute. None of its baseline/assisted times has been observed. The 40-minute Opus report in Stage 1 belongs to that practitioner, not this client.

| Event | Assumed weekly volume | Baseline → assisted human minutes, including review | Hypothesised weekly capacity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Original-video handoff preparation/context recovery | 2 | 25 → 10 | 30 minutes |
| Accepted short-derivative selection and correction | 8 | 10 → 6 | 32 minutes |
| Re-entering/reconciling approved release metadata | 10 content items | 6 → 3 | 30 minutes |
| **Total, unmeasured** | — | — | **92 minutes/week ≈ 1.53 hours/week** |

These assumed weekly rates are a simple four-week production model, not an exact conversion of every calendar month into weeks. Additional platform destinations, rights disputes, caption corrections and failed jobs must be added to both conditions rather than omitted. Zero saved time and negative net time are valid outcomes.

**Pilot baseline:** select matched original videos and derivatives of comparable duration/complexity, using the same people, tools, rights and acceptance standard. Record all preparation, review, rework, context recovery, notification chasing, exceptions and final release checks. Separate elapsed waiting from active human effort. Keep the old workflow for comparison; log missing permissions and rejected candidate clips rather than deleting them from the dataset.

**Quality gates:** no unauthorised account action; no stale asset approved as current; no missing grant treated as permission; no changed caption/timebase meaning; no incorrect disclosure or materially poorer creative output. A critical failure blocks adoption regardless of minutes saved. Paid time released is capacity, **not payroll savings**. Only realised reductions in paid overtime/contractor costs or observed incremental contribution margin enter net cash.

## Migration cost, including the systems not being migrated

**[analysis] First migration is a fixed-scope acceptance exercise:** export one board and one redacted message thread; map IDs/permissions; import a bounded production with originals and licence evidence; test two review revisions; verify a native/manual release receipt; train the owner and one reviewer; test export and restore. Preserve native project files, prior review history and existing accounting authority. Do not require a wholesale account or course migration to test the wedge.

As a sensitivity example only, **24 hours × $50 = $1,200 one-time change cost**. That is not a discovered market rate, estimate validated against implementation, or invoice. Adding it to the care scenario gives **−$1,382.40 first-year cash difference** before still-unknown costs. A billed build fee would be additional. Time or quality value must therefore be demonstrated separately rather than hidden inside a rosy subscription total.

Lock-in tests follow Stage 3: which Airtable relations/automations survive export; which Frame.io links/comments remain readable; which scheduled posts require reconnecting accounts; whether old approval maps to exact media; which design/voice/project assets can be reused; whether client and collaborator licences allow the proposed route. Leave financial records in the accountant-approved system unless an authorised migration is separately accepted.

## Vertical economics and the second deployment

**[analysis] Eligible market population is unknown.** Total YouTube channels, platform users, subscriber counts or global creator-economy headlines are not a count of original faceless educational/documentary producer-owners with this workflow and willingness to maintain a VPS. Record `eligible_operators: null` rather than multiply a global headline by an assumed conversion rate.

The reusable opportunity is the same acceptance fixture, object contracts, licence/edition checklist, package versions, adapter tests and operating runbook. The **second** deployment should not repeat the full market sweep or rebuild the object model. An illustrative second-install allowance is **8 setup hours × $50 = $400**, plus that client's actual data migration, training and annual operating/support costs. This is a target to falsify, not a proven one-click deployment cost. Different platform permissions, voice languages, editor formats or statutory-accounting requirements can destroy that reuse and must be priced explicitly.

A simple investment test is `number_of_accepted_deployments × measured_contribution_per_deployment − first_assembly_engineering − shared_maintenance`. Every unknown remains null. Do not reject the segment merely because an individual operator has a small software budget; equally, do not turn possible scale into fabricated total addressable revenue.

## Verdict and falsifier

**[analysis] YES to a bounded versioned-handoff pilot; NO to claiming that the complete creator business can already run autonomously on one VPS or that this research proves cash savings.** Preserve the shipped script/growth asset. Lift production/review and media primitives. Own the evidence-bearing joins and approval boundary.

The thesis is bad if the real workflow is already cheap and coherent; reviewers reject the new handoff; per-client support and migration exceed demonstrated benefit; platform restrictions remove the useful integration; full rights/editor fidelity cannot be preserved; or assisted human effort including corrections is not lower. The reuse thesis fails if the second deployment requires essentially the same bespoke work as the first. A non-positive observed cash result rejects a cash-saving offer unless the client separately authorises a non-cash control/quality objective.
