# Companies — Music creators and recording artists

Research observed **2026-09-13**. **10 Tier-1 companies; 10 Tier-2 offerings; 10 workflow stages.** Company research precedes this continuation's new upstream repository sweep. Prices below are source observations, not quotes for NM. [analysis]

## Scope and interpretation

NM's original specification is unavailable; the owning README says creator/artist workflow, not professional music editing. Geography, operating system, existing licences, revenue, collaborators and actual spending remain unknown. The comparison operator is a proposed independent beatmaker who also releases recordings. This is a low-priority consumer favour, not an admitted commercial build. [analysis — source: https://github.com/sisodias/siso-industry-packs/blob/main/packs/music_creation/README.md]

`[vendor]` means the supplier's description, not independently tested performance. `[practitioner]` means a particular user's report, not prevalence or a reproduced defect. `[analysis]` marks our classification, proposed operating boundary or calculation. The two tiers classify the capability ceiling and small-operator purchase floor; they are not a company valuation ranking. Only music-relevant product lines are profiled. No vendor's internal staffing or implementation was observed. [analysis]

## What the tier gap actually is

The floor already includes capable low-cost or free DAWs, distribution subscriptions and a local project manager. Therefore, “small creators cannot afford professional audio software” is not a sufficient thesis. The difficult gap is continuity: which source files produced this bounce, whose contribution/split was approved, whether the sample receipt is retained, and which distributor accepted the final metadata. Creative rendering, commercial catalogues, human judgement and platform relationships remain separate capabilities. This is an inference from the products above, not a measured statement about NM. [analysis]

Large vendors also sell infrastructure and services that are inappropriate to emulate on a laptop. A local binder may reduce re-entry and handoff mistakes; it cannot promise Spotify delivery, publishing collection, audience reach, or arbitrary plug-in-compatible project playback just because its own database is local. [analysis; relevant boundaries: https://artists.spotify.com/get-started ; https://saltadosessions.com/preview-projects-without-opening-daw ; https://www.songtrust.com/pricing]

## STACK TAX — arithmetic, not claimed savings

For one eligible US-priced comparison seat, buying **every distinct priced tool in the stage table**: [analysis, inputs sourced above]

```
Recurring normalised annual list/renewal basis:
Splice          12.99 × 12 = 155.88
Workspace        7.00 × 12 =  84.00
Notion          10.00 × 12 = 120.00
Airtable        20.00 × 12 = 240.00
QuickBooks      38.00 × 12 = 456.00
BandLab Pro renewal        = 149.00
SoundCloud Pro             =  99.00
TuneCore Rising            =  24.99
Apple Creator Studio       = 129.00
                            --------
Annual recurring basket    = USD 1,457.87

Initial licences/fees:
FL Producer 179 + REAPER 60 + Saltado Pro 44 + Songtrust 100
                            = USD 383.00

First-year cash basket using BandLab's 99 introductory annual price:
1,457.87 - 50 + 383          = USD 1,790.87
```

Variable fees, tax/FX not already included by a cited price, sample-clearance payments, payment volume, marketing budgets, hardware/storage, human mixing/mastering and the owner's time are **excluded and unmeasured**, not zero. Bandcamp and Stripe must not both be charged to the same transaction unless that actual payment route incurs both. Publishing-administration percentages apply to their relevant collection bases, not the artist's entire turnover. Existing perpetual purchases are sunk spend, not a future annual saving. [analysis; fee bases: https://stripe.com/pricing ; https://get.bandcamp.help/en/articles/15263193-what-are-bandcamp-s-fees ; https://www.songtrust.com/pricing]

This basket intentionally includes three competing DAWs and overlapping planning/distribution services. It is an **overbuy ceiling**, not an efficient stack, NM's observed expenditure or a sum the proposed app could eliminate. A full sum for every plan in the twenty-company catalogue is **not computable from this evidence**: some prices are regional, usage-based or unverified. [analysis]

The public observed-value model requires actual cancelled spend, customer baseline, comparative quality and complete operating/change costs. Those inputs are absent here, so realised annual cash savings and net value remain **null / unmeasured**.
