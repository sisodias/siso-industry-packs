# The person — an independent beatmaker who also releases recordings

**Observed 2026-09-13. Stage 1 research, not an interview.** Eight source-linked practitioner complaints; eight workflow-distinct segments. Evidence labels: `[vendor]` supplier statement; `[practitioner]` individual user report; `[analysis]` our proposed design or interpretation.

## What we know, and what we do not

The owning record identifies **NM**, a friend who makes beats and wants AI help. It explicitly says the original specification was lost, this is a **low-priority consumer personal favour**, and the creator/artist workflow supersedes the older editing-for-hire framing. Source: https://github.com/sisodias/siso-industry-packs/blob/main/packs/music_creation/README.md [analysis of supplied scope]

There is no source establishing NM's DAW, computer, country, income, clients, collaborators, social channels, working hours, plug-ins, files, spending or desired AI function. This chapter does not restore those facts by imagination. No private account, personal recording, message, payment or device was inspected. [analysis]

**Proposed primary operator:** one independent beatmaker/recording artist working on their own laptop, composing instrumental tracks and sometimes collaborating with a vocalist. They own creative and release decisions. For the hypothetical paid route, the buyer receives a licensed beat or an agreed recording deliverable; for a self-release, the durable output is an approved recording and its release package. The balance between these routes must be confirmed with NM. This is a concrete design scenario, not a claim that NM runs a studio or already earns royalties. [analysis]

The output is not “a music website.” A site can sell a beat; the working system must connect the reference, project, sample provenance, contribution, revision, approval, release and eventual statement. The usefulness of connecting these objects is the thesis to test, not a demonstrated demand. [analysis]

## A proposed Tuesday — testable, not observed

Times below are a scenario used to expose handoffs. Frequency and duration are **unmeasured**, not a timesheet or promise of returned hours. The existing DAW is retained. [analysis]

| Time | What happens in the scenario | What persists / who is awaited |
|---|---|---|
| 09:00 | Read a vocalist's email or message, listen to a reference and capture a voice-note idea. Do not send an automated commitment. | A user-selected conversation excerpt and draft creative brief; wait for the other person's intended use and deadline. |
| 09:30 | Audition drums or a loop; identify whether it is an original recording, a purchased sample or material needing permission. | Local audio plus its source/receipt reference; unresolved provenance stays unresolved. |
| 10:00–12:00 | Compose, arrange and record in the current DAW; save the native project. | Project state, audio/MIDI and a rough bounce, not merely the name “final.” |
| 12:00 | Create an explicit checkpoint and inspect referenced media before copying a backup. | A manifest and backup location; a copied file is not a successful restore test. |
| 13:00 | Send or receive a bounce for feedback; record what the collaborator actually approved. | Time-linked notes and the exact revision they heard; wait for review and contribution/split agreement. |
| 14:00 | Optionally separate or transcribe audio for an authorised purpose; compare the result to the original. | Input hash, tool/model version, settings and derived asset. Processing does not create permission to release. |
| 15:00 | Return to the DAW, make the mix decision, export aligned stems and a master. | A human-approved render; technical QC is distinct from taste. |
| 16:00 | Prepare titles, contributors, artwork, identifiers and evidence of rights. | Release package linked to a specific recording revision; wait for missing approvals. |
| 17:00 | Use the chosen distributor's portal only after approval; record its acknowledgement. | Submission reference and any rejection; distribution is not an offline database operation. |
| 17:30 | Reconcile a received statement or payment, then prepare a fan update or storefront change for approval. | Original statement plus reconciled lines and consent-aware follow-up. |

The scenario's media-packaging distinction is grounded in Ableton's documentation: project collection is a specific operation and does not collect third-party plug-ins. The distributor boundary is grounded in Spotify's artist guidance. These mechanisms support a design investigation, not proof that NM encounters them weekly. [vendor] https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/209775645-Collect-All-and-Save ; https://artists.spotify.com/get-started

## Eight practitioner complaints — quotations, provenance and limits

These are **eight distinct public source pages**, not eight NM interviews or a representative survey. Several are historical. They establish that particular people reported friction; they do not establish a current defect, its frequency, or willingness to pay. Where only an indexed excerpt was retrievable, that limitation is explicit. Brief excerpts preserve the original wording. [analysis]

### P01 — subscription-credit continuity

> “don’t let your credits stack, as it will all be worthless if you don’t stay subscribed.”

**[practitioner]** Reviewer “Splice is Shady,” **2025-11-18**, Splice iOS listing. https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/splice-make-music-now/id1108532275

**[analysis]** This supports a concern about unused credits and continuity, not the proposition that downloaded sample rights disappear. The same review contains a broader rights claim that should not be adopted. Splice says downloaded samples remain licensed after cancellation while unused-credit rules differ. Keep downloaded files and licensing evidence distinct from an account's credit balance. **[vendor]** https://support.splice.com/en/articles/8652638-if-i-cancel-my-subscription-do-i-lose-my-samples-and-credits

### P02 — distribution remains an external authority

> “My entire DistroKid catalog was suddenly removed from YouTube Music”

**[practitioner]** ShaneLents, **2025-11-12**, YouTube Music help community; indexed post text retrieved, direct page subsequently exposed only a shell. https://support.google.com/youtubemusic/thread/387216488/youtube-music-removed-all-my-distrokid-releases-%E2%80%93-need-partner-operations-review?hl=en

**[analysis]** A reported takedown, not an independently verified reason or evidence that every distributor has the same defect. A local app can retain submission/acknowledgement evidence and help assemble an escalation; it cannot guarantee reinstatement.

### P03 — metadata entry and registration friction

> “was extremely tedious and difficult. It took several attempts.”

**[practitioner]** Music Library Report discussion of publisher registration and album/track metadata. Author and post date were not exposed in the retrieved indexed excerpt; direct page retrieval failed. **Historical date unknown; weak freshness.** https://musiclibraryreport.com/forums/topic/should-i-become-a-harry-fox-publishing-affiliate/

**[analysis]** This is a lead for mapping repeated entry and rejected metadata. Do not reuse historical fee or eligibility statements from this discussion as current publishing advice. The proposed module prepares evidence-backed fields; a receiving organisation still decides acceptance.

### P04 — a missing-file warning without an obvious missing sound

> “I have no idea what was missing”

**[practitioner]** Krispy, **May 2010**, production forum thread; indexed historical excerpt, direct retrieval later failed. https://dnbforum.com/threads/ableton-problem-samples-are-missing.100399/

**[analysis]** Old usability evidence, not a current Ableton defect claim. The lasting question is whether an operator can identify a project's referenced files and restore them. Current vendor guidance separately documents missing-media handling and cloud-storage considerations. **[vendor]** https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/209070829-Missing-media-files

### P05 — coordinating concurrent project versions

> “Can only one of us work on a version at a time”

**[practitioner]** projectink / Jeremy, **2022-09-15**, Ableton forum, describing three collaborators and V1/V2/V3 Dropbox folders. https://forum.ableton.com/viewtopic.php?t=245828

**[analysis]** A concrete coordination question, not evidence that today's tools provide no collaboration features. The proposed binder gives each reviewed bounce an identity and records who is editing; it must not pretend to merge arbitrary DAW binaries or plug-in state.

### P06 — keeping sound dependencies aligned

> “In a quandary about how to manage Ableton's Factory Packs”

**[practitioner]** ClintGoss, **2024-05-11**, factory-pack version-management discussion involving several machines; indexed excerpt retrieved, direct page subsequently failed. https://forum.ableton.com/viewtopic.php?p=1824674

**[analysis]** A dependency-management lead. It does not prove that NM has several machines or that one product can redistribute proprietary packs. Record what a project requires and what is installed; never package somebody else's licensed content as if it were ours.

### P07 — fragile links to controller extensions

> “All links are dead and he hasn't logged in in years.”

**[practitioner]** 4T, **2016-03-21**, an Ableton controller discussion. https://forum.ableton.com/viewtopic.php?sid=e015a01a5121c4a460ea443e32861190&start=30&t=81395

**[analysis]** Historical link continuity, not current project abandonment. The same discussion records a subsequent re-upload by the author; preserve that counterevidence. The design lesson is to retain permitted dependency/version references and recovery instructions rather than treating an old forum URL as a durable installation plan.

### P08 — the import boundary can fail before the AI does anything

> “I tried every way to import a song but nothing works.”

**[practitioner]** Sachinbhavsarin, **2025-11-21**, Stem Split AI Vocal Remover iOS review, retrieved in indexed listing text. https://apps.apple.com/in/app/stem-split-ai-vocal-remover/id6742150522

**[analysis]** A single app-specific failure report, not a benchmark of separation quality or a defect in our eventual component. Later listing/version notes describe import/reliability changes, so the report cannot be labelled an unresolved September 2026 defect. Test import, permissions and export before evaluating model quality.

## Why this segment, and what would falsify the person model

The first segment preserves the only surviving specific intent: making beats with AI assistance. It also permits a narrow test connecting creative assets to a release without requiring a fictional studio, payroll or label. But the research does **not** establish that NM needs a business operating system. [analysis; owning scope: https://github.com/sisodias/siso-industry-packs/blob/main/packs/music_creation/README.md]

A single observed project should answer: what was the last frustrating task; what software and machine were used; where did the reference arrive; which files and licences were needed; who approved the result; and whether anything was paid or released. Record elapsed and active minutes separately. Until that observation exists, operator frequency, current stack tax, acceptable migration effort and hours returned remain **unmeasured**. [analysis]

**Falsifier:** if the real ask is simply one-click stem separation inside NM's existing workflow, or if existing project management already resolves the handoff pain, a broad super-app is the wrong response. Compare the actual task against existing tools before authorising implementation. Saltado already advertises a free local project catalogue and a low-cost Pro tier; a catalogue-only clone has no established wedge. **[vendor]** https://www.saltadosessions.com/ **[analysis]** The comparison outcome, not the size of an OSS list, should decide whether to build.
