## Who this blueprint is for

[source supplied by project] This remains the medium-priority restaurant client job for AC. The [existing project brief](README.md) establishes a marketing-site asset, not an operating system. The website already holds landing/menu/reviews/about functionality; do not re-research or rebuild that ground.

[analysis — explicit design scenario, not AC biography] Design for the **owner-manager of one casual-dining restaurant that serves seated guests and prepares takeaway from the same kitchen**. Use 60 seats, 20 paid staff and one site solely as sizing assumptions. The owner works between the front desk, kitchen pass, stock room and back-office laptop. Their customer buys a correctly fulfilled meal and an acceptable dining/collection experience, not an internal software workflow. Supplier queries, staff cover, missing tickets and settlement disputes compete with that deliverable.

## A concrete Tuesday to test, not an observed diary

Every time and activity below is **[analysis / proposed observation schedule]**. No duration is counted as measured work or savings. The workflow families are supported by the product map in [03-companies.md](03-companies.md); the specific sequence is a design hypothesis.

| Time | The owner's work and handoff | Durable evidence to recover in a real observation |
|---|---|---|
| 08:00 | Check unanswered messages and yesterday's unresolved close; distinguish a table enquiry from an accepted reservation. | Original message, owner, reply, reservation/order reference, unresolved payout difference. |
| 09:00 | Resolve an absence with a shift lead; obtain acceptance of cover rather than merely sending a notification. | Availability, proposed swap, manager approval, worker acknowledgement and versioned rota. |
| 10:00 | Receive a supplier delivery with the chef. Compare ordered cases, delivered units, invoice price and substitutions. | Purchase order, delivery receipt, pack conversion, discrepancy and requested credit. |
| 11:00 | Review prep quantities and ingredient shortages before publishing availability. Complete opening checks. | Recipe version/yield, prep quantity, stock position, human-recorded safety result and corrective action. |
| 12:00 | Start lunch: the host handles seating while the owner resolves questions and takeaway timing. | Table/party assignment, accepted order, promised collection window and source conversation. |
| 13:00 | Work the pass: reconcile changed modifiers, a printer problem or an order that has not been acknowledged. | Kitchen ticket version, station acknowledgement, reprint reason and exception owner. |
| 14:00 | Finish lunch handoffs and check whether a courier collected the correct order. | Ready/collected timestamps, courier reference and proof of handoff, without unrelated guest data. |
| 15:00 | Enter late supplier credits, count a disputed item and review menu cost changes with the chef. | Receipt/credit match, count amendment, effective ingredient cost and approved menu change. |
| 16:00 | Confirm the evening rota, reservations and large-party requirements. Brief the next shift. | Handover checklist, acknowledged staffing, reservation changes and reviewed guest instructions. |
| 17:00 | Open dinner service; verify sold-out items agree across the till and ordering channels. | Channel menu/version, failed updates and approved availability state. |
| 18:00–21:00 | Prioritise service, kitchen exceptions, arrivals and collections. Do not make back-office automation a prerequisite for taking payment. | Order-to-ticket-to-payment lineage, amendments and service recovery decisions. |
| 22:00 | Close with the supervisor; identify unresolved cash/card/marketplace differences and approve the accounting handoff. | Close batch, expected vs received settlement, tips/refunds/fees, approved export and unresolved queue. |
| Weekly review | Approve timesheets, reconcile supplier/accounting records, review consented follow-ups and revise the next rota. | Accepted payroll period, reconciled invoice/settlement records, suppression lists and an accountable review. |

## Eight segments that actually change the workflow

This segmentation is **[analysis]**, grounded in the different product families in [Stage 3](03-companies.md), not a measured market census.

| Segment | Workflow difference that changes the system |
|---|---|
| **Mixed dine-in and takeaway casual dining — primary** | One kitchen receives seated and off-premise demand; reservations, modifiers, timing, stock and settlement must agree. |
| Counter-service / quick-service | Queue throughput, modifiers, kiosks and collection dominate; table reservations may be irrelevant. |
| Delivery-only / virtual brands | Several channel menus and payouts may share ingredients and production capacity; courier handoff and reconciliation dominate. |
| Fine dining / tasting menus | Course timing, deposits, dietary review, seat pacing and high-touch guest records outweigh rapid counter throughput. |
| Bars / pubs with food | Open tabs, drinks measures, split payment and stock variance require different controls from a recipe-only kitchen. |
| Cafes / bakeries | Batch production, shelf life, morning demand and waste matter more than evening table pacing. |
| Catering / private events | Enquiry→quote→deposit→event run sheet→final invoice; headcount changes and off-site logistics are first-class objects. |
| Small groups / commissary-led operators | Shared purchasing, transfers, recipes and roles across sites; local operational independence and consolidated reporting both matter. |

[analysis] Choose the mixed dine-in/takeaway operator because it exercises the cross-system joins explicitly named in the brief without starting with multi-site tenancy or enterprise hardware replacement. This is a research choice, not an assertion about AC's actual service model. Confirm that model before a pilot. Never use the scenario to silently commit AC to US payroll, a particular POS, or an all-at-once migration.
