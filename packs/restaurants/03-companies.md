# 03 — The companies and the cost of a fragmented restaurant stack

Observed **2026-09-13**. Research, not a procurement recommendation or a client quote. **10 Tier-1 companies; 10 Tier-2 offerings.** The tiers describe the integrated capability ceiling and small-operator purchasing options, not an audited company-size ranking. A company can supply both markets.

## Basis and unknowns

[analysis] AC's country, staff count, installed systems, transaction volume, contracts and invoices have not been supplied. The pricing reference case is therefore explicitly hypothetical: **one US location, 60 seats, 20 paid employees, one POS software licence, one kitchen display, three office users and two Airtable editors**. These are design assumptions, not facts about AC. Dollar prices below are the displayed US-dollar offers where specified; tax, payment processing, hardware and onboarding are separate unless the source explicitly includes them. No promotional introductory discount is annualised. UK delivery brands in the brief remain a separate integration requirement; US prices do not establish UK availability or AC's jurisdiction.

[source supplied by project] The existing asset covers the marketing site, not restaurant operations. Preserve it rather than replace it. See the [existing restaurant brief](README.md). No private client source was re-audited for this pack.

**Reading convention:** product and price descriptions are **[vendor]** assertions; review complaints are **[practitioner]**, indexed in [01-person.md](01-person.md); architecture, operational implications and recommendations are **[analysis]**. A listed integration is not a tested integration. Where a plan does not expose a price or entitlement clearly, the answer is **quote required / unknown**, not zero.

## The gap between the tiers

[analysis] The ceiling is not another till. It joins guest identity and orders to kitchen state, recipes, purchasing, labour and settlement evidence. The small-operator floor contains capable individual tools, including zero-subscription options. Our opportunity is **less retyping and fewer unresolved cross-system exceptions**, not an unsupported claim that small restaurants have no software.

[analysis] Four joins determine whether the assembly is useful: conversation→reservation/order; supplier item/pack→ingredient/recipe cost; sold modifier/portion→stock depletion; order/refund/fee→payout/bank entry. A collection of tabs does not solve those joins. An integration service may remain cheaper and safer than replacing the point of sale.

## The unglamorous systems are requirements, not afterthoughts

[vendor] Google Workspace's US business page lists Starter at **$7/user/month with annual commitment**, including office communication/storage; the standard rate is used, not a date-dependent promotion. Three office users cost 3×7×12=$252/year. [Source](https://workspace.google.com/business/). Airtable Team is **$20/collaborator/month annually** or $24 monthly; two paid collaborators cost $480/year. The official plan description covers forms, interfaces, automations and API limits. [Source](https://support.airtable.com/articles/2277136852-airtable-plans-overview).

[vendor] The public [StockCount restaurant inventory template](https://stockcount.io/tools/restaurant-inventory-template) describes Excel/Google Sheets fields for quantities, unit cost, inventory value and reorder needs. This pass read the page, not the downloaded workbook or an operator-owned sheet. [analysis] A chef-maintained supplier/ingredient sheet would specify item aliases, pack units, count locations, invoice dates and reorder thresholds. That ownership and schema must be elicited from AC, not invented as observed usage.

[analysis] Airtable/Notion/Monday/ClickUp and shared calendars are interview targets for exception boards, private-event requests, prep checklists and supplier reminders. No evidence supplied here establishes which AC uses. A custom board's fields, views, automations and permissions are requirements to recover, not a reason to add another generic app. Microsoft Excel, QuickBooks and Sage are alternatives to investigate in the actual region; their licences are **not** costed or asserted installed in this reference basket.

[vendor] The [WhatsApp Business app](https://business.whatsapp.com/products/business-app) advertises direct customer conversations, business information and response tools. [analysis] That does not establish an unrestricted API or eliminate platform charges. Phone, SMS, Instagram, cash, existing calendars, paper tickets, whiteboards and staff memory stay in the workflow inventory; their cash/time costs require a client baseline. Zero new software licence is not zero migration/training cost. Payment processing remains an external service, not a VPS-hosted capability.
